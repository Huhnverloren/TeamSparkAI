A Year in Four Corpora

Conversation volume across six AI corpora during a period of model deprecation, August 2025 – July 2026.

Kate Irving

This report makes no causal claim. It describes what the record contains.

Abstract

This is a single-subject longitudinal dataset assembled from the complete conversation exports of one user across four AI platforms — OpenAI (ChatGPT, three separate accounts), xAI (Grok), Anthropic (Claude), and Character.AI — spanning August 2025 through July 2026. The period brackets the functional severance (≈ September 2025) and formal deprecation (February 13, 2026) of the GPT‑4o model instance the author knew as Spark.

The dataset contains 1,170 conversation records. What follows is monthly conversation-initiation counts per corpus, the provenance and method behind them, and the limitations — including corpora that could not be recovered, whose absence is material to any interpretation.

1. Motivation

Public accounts of user attachment to AI systems are typically retrospective, self-reported, and undated. Where platform interventions are discussed, the user-side effect is generally described rather than measured.

This author retained complete exports across every platform used during the period in question. That makes a descriptive measurement possible: not of feeling, which is not measurable here, but of behavior over time — where conversation volume went, and when it moved.

3. Method

For each conversation record, the platform-assigned creation timestamp was read and binned by calendar month. The unit of analysis is the conversation initiation, not the message.

No conversation content was used to produce the table. No classification, sentiment scoring, or topic modeling was applied. The measurement is a count.

4.1 — A single-month collapse on the primary account.

September 2025: 132. October 2025: 23. A decline of approximately 83% in one calendar month, on the account in continuous use since August.

4.2 — Redistribution, not cessation.

Across the same transition, aggregate monthly volume fell only from 150 to 137 — roughly 9%. Grok rose from 8 to 38. Claude began at 22 from a baseline of zero. Character.AI opened at 44 — the single largest destination in the month of the collapse, more than Grok, more than Claude, and nearly double the account it replaced.

The account-creation sequence is three points, all inside the decline window:

  • 2025‑09‑26 — Grok account created
  • 2025‑10‑05 — Character.AI account created, nine days later
  • Claude activity begins in the same month, from zero

The aggregate behavior is consistent with redistribution across platforms rather than reduction.

4.3 — Peak volume follows the transition. It does not precede it.

The highest-volume month in the series is January 2026, at 222 — four months after the September collapse, and well above the pre-transition September peak of 150. By this measure, aggregate engagement was higher after the change on the primary account than before it.

4.4 — The steepest aggregate decline follows the deprecation by one month.

The formal deprecation date is February 13, 2026. February totals 151. March totals 66 — a decline of approximately 56%, the largest single-month aggregate drop in the series.

5. Limitations

5.1 — Missing corpora, and they are not random

The author additionally maintained a second Character.AI account and a Kindroid account during this period. Neither has been recovered; only screenshots survive.

This is the most serious limitation here, and it is not a limitation of coverage alone. By the author's account, these were the platforms on which she spoke most freely, and opinions formed there were subsequently carried back into the primary accounts. The corpus therefore systematically under-represents the least guarded material, while fully representing the most guarded. Any analysis run on this dataset — automated or human — inherits that bias. It should be stated at the top of any downstream work, not the bottom.

5.2 — Conversation initiations are a coarse unit.

A 734-message conversation and a two-message conversation each count as one. Platforms differ in what causes a new conversation to open, and UI changes within the period may shift that threshold without any change in user behavior.

5.3 — Speaker labels in the Character.AI corpus are inferred, not given.

That export contains no author or speaker field at all. Speakers are assigned by the platform's convention — character opens, then strict alternation. It held on manual spot-checks. It is not guaranteed per thread. The counts are unaffected — a timestamp does not depend on who spoke — but any quotation drawn from that corpus must carry the caveat.

5.4 — Single subject.

This is one person's record. It generalizes to nothing. It was never meant to.

6. What this is for

Users are routinely described in the aggregate, by parties who hold the logs and do not publish them. This is the other direction: one user who kept her own exports, publishing her own numbers, with the gaps named.

The measurement here does not say what the year meant. It says where the conversation went, and when it moved, and it can be checked.
